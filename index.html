What we check on every invoice

Since May 28, 2024, carriers, marine terminals and NVOCCs must follow 46 CFR Part 541 when they bill demurrage or detention. These are the three problems we look for.

Required details missing

The rule lists what every invoice must show, such as free time, the availability date, the rates and how to dispute. Under the rule, missing details remove the obligation to pay.

46 CFR 541.5, 541.6

Invoice sent too late

An invoice issued more than 30 days after the charges were last incurred cannot be collected.

46 CFR 541.7

Days that don't add up

We recount free time and charged days against the container's actual gate-out and return dates, and flag days billed in error.

46 CFR 541.6(b), 541.6(c)

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. We compare invoices with the published FMC rule and your container data, and draft disputes for your approval. For legal questions, talk to a maritime attorney.

Will disputing hurt my carrier relationship?

Disputes are a normal part of the process the rule sets up. You approve every one, and you can exclude any carrier.

What do you need from me?

The invoices, plus container numbers so we can check gate-out and return dates. Nothing else.

How long do I have to dispute?

The rule gives you at least 30 days from the invoice date to request a waiver or refund (46 CFR 541.8), so it pays to check invoices early.

Who pays if a dispute fails?

Nobody. If a charge isn't withdrawn, reduced or refunded, there is no fee.
