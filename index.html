Why D&D invoices often fail the rule

Since May 28, 2024, carriers, marine terminals and NVOCCs must follow 46 CFR Part 541 when they bill demurrage or detention. Three things come up again and again.

Required details are missing

Every invoice must show items such as the free time, the container availability date, the rates applied, why you are the liable party, and how to dispute. If any are missing, you have no obligation to pay it.

46 CFR 541.5, 541.6

The invoice came too late

A billing party that issues an invoice more than 30 days after the charges were last incurred loses the right to collect them.

46 CFR 541.7

The days don't add up

We recalculate free time and charged days against the container's actual gate-out and return dates, and flag days billed in error.

46 CFR 541.6(b), 541.6(c)

What the rule requires on every invoice

A summary of 46 CFR 541.6. You have at least 30 days from the invoice date to request a waiver or refund (541.8), so it pays to check early.

CategoryExamples of required items
IdentificationBill of lading and container numbers, port of discharge, why you are the liable party
TimingInvoice and due dates, free time in days with start and end dates, container availability date, the exact dates charged
RatesTotal due, the tariff rule or contract applied, the specific rates
DisputesA contact, a web link describing what to submit, and the dispute timeframes
CertificationsThat the charges follow FMC rules and the billing party's own performance did not cause them

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. We compare invoices with the published FMC rule and your container data, and draft disputes for your approval. For legal questions, talk to a maritime attorney.

Will disputing hurt my carrier relationship?

Disputes are a normal part of the process the rule sets up. You approve every one, and you can exclude any carrier.

What do you need from me?

The invoices, plus container numbers so we can check gate-out and return dates. Nothing else.

Who pays if a dispute fails?

Nobody. If a charge isn't withdrawn, reduced or refunded, there is no fee.
